\begin{abstract}
While static functional realizations map finite discrete arrays into infinite-dimensional spaces, dynamical processes in high-dimensional data, network physics, and optimization require a non-equilibrium geometric foundation. In this treatise, we establish a rigorous framework for \emph{Geometric Flows, Non-Local Partial Differential Equations, and Variational Dynamics} operating on matrix and tensor manifolds, as well as their infinite-dimensional continuum limits. 

We systematically investigate: (i) canonical Riemannian metrics on matrix manifolds---including the affine-invariant geometry of the positive definite cone $\mathcal{S}_{++}^n$, Bures-Wasserstein optimal transport geometries, and the embedded Riemannian submanifold structure of fixed-rank matrix varieties $\mathcal{M}_r$; (ii) continuous Hamiltonian and gradient dynamics, recalling that the Toda lattice flow interpolates the QR algorithm applied to $e^{A_0}$ via isospectral Lax-pair equations, and projected gradient flows on $\mathcal{M}_r$ either exist for all time or reach a lower-rank matrix in finite time; (iii) \emph{Graphon Partial Differential Equations}: for the non-local graphon heat equation we give the exact solution, prove contraction of the cut norm, and show that discontinuities persist (no parabolic smoothing); and (iv) curvature-driven evolutions, namely an \emph{Ollivier-Wasserstein Ricci Flow} on graphons (with decay and growth estimates conditional on curvature bounds) and the formal coupling of the Coarea formula to level-set Mean Curvature Flow. Finally, we give a formal derivation, via the McLachlan variational principle, of imaginary-time Schr\"odinger evolution projected onto continuous Matrix Product States as an energy-non-increasing gradient flow for a gauge-degenerate metric. We also prove that the continuum limit of contracted tensor-ring chains recovers non-Abelian Wilson-loop holonomies with an explicit $\mathcal{O}(1/k)$ error bound.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: From Static Realizations to Geometric Dynamics}
\label{sec:intro}
% =========================================================================

In the companion work \cite{silvafilho2026functional}, we investigated the static embedding of discrete multilinear arrays into function spaces via Functional Realization Operators $\Phi \colon \mathcal{T}^k(V) \to \mathcal{F}(\Omega)$, and studied quantities that are not spectral invariants (Sobolev energies, total variation, Morse spectra, and graphon cut limits). 

However, in computational mathematics, statistical physics, and non-convex optimization, matrices and tensors rarely remain static. Rather, they evolve continuously in time $t \ge 0$ under continuous dynamical laws:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:matrix_ode_general}
    \frac{\dif A(t)}{\dif t} = \mathcal{V}(A(t)), \quad A(0) = A_0,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{V}$ is a vector field on an ambient matrix space or tangent to a curved sub-manifold $\mathcal{M}$. Classical examples include continuous-time neural learning, natural gradient descent in machine learning, quantum state evolution via Lindbladian master equations, and the continuous interpolation of numerical linear algebra algorithms such as the QR algorithm and power iterations.

Despite the ubiquity of these dynamical processes, an overarching geometric and PDE-theoretic formulation unifying discrete matrix flows with continuum partial differential equations remains largely fragmented. Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item \textbf{Curvature and Invariance on Matrix Cones:} The Euclidean Frobenius metric $\inner{U}{V}_F = \Tr(U^T V)$ is flat, yet the space of positive definite covariance matrices $\mathcal{S}_{++}^n$ has non-positive sectional curvature under the affine-invariant metric, which alters the convergence-rate constants of Riemannian gradient flows relative to their Euclidean counterparts.
    \item \textbf{Rank Degeneracy and Boundary Singularities:} The variety of real $m \times n$ matrices of fixed rank $r < \min(m, n)$, denoted $\mathcal{M}_r$, is an embedded Riemannian submanifold. However, its closure $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_r$ contains matrices of rank $< r$ as singular boundary strata, leading to curvature blow-ups and loss of tangent projection well-posedness in matrix completion algorithms.
    \item \textbf{The Continuum Limit of Evolving Networks ($n \to \infty$):} While graphons $W \colon [0, 1]^2 \to [0, 1]$ characterize static dense graph limits in the cut metric, dynamic processes on large networks require solving non-local partial differential equations $\partial_t W(t, x, y) = \mathcal{F}(W(t, \cdot, \cdot))$.
    \item \textbf{Curvature-Driven Matrix Smoothing:} While Ricci flows $\partial_t g = -2 \Ric(g)$ deform Riemannian metrics to uniformize geometry, their matrix and graphon analogues---driven by Ollivier-Wasserstein coarse curvature---remain to be systematized.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Core Contributions}
In this treatise, we develop the differential geometry and PDE theory of matrix and tensor flows. We prove:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
    \item The equivalence of Toda lattice Hamiltonian flows with the continuous QR eigenvalue algorithm under isospectral commutator dynamics (Theorem~\ref{thm:toda_qr});
    \item Rank preservation and energy decay for projected Riemannian gradient flows on $\mathcal{M}_r$, and convergence to a matrix of lower rank when the existence time is finite (Theorem~\ref{thm:rank_preservation});
    \item Global well-posedness, contraction of the cut norm, and persistence of discontinuities with amplitude $e^{-2t}$ for non-local Graphon Heat Equations $\partial_t W = \Delta_\otimes W$ (Theorems~\ref{thm:graphon_heat_existence} and \ref{thm:heat_smoothing});
    \item Exponential decay of negatively curved bridges in graphon Ricci flows, conditional on curvature bounds along the flow (Proposition~\ref{thm:ricci_neckpinch});
    \item Perimeter dissipation of level sets under smooth level-set Mean Curvature Flow via the Coarea identity (Proposition~\ref{thm:mean_curvature_coarea}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Riemannian Metrics and Geometry on Matrix and Tensor Manifolds}
\label{sec:riemannian_geometry}
% =========================================================================

Let $\R^{m \times n}$ denote the space of real matrices. We endow matrix spaces and subsets thereof with non-Euclidean Riemannian structures.

\subsection{The Cone of Symmetric Positive Definite Matrices}
\label{subsec:cone_spd}
The set $\mathcal{S}_{++}^n \coloneqq \{A \in \operatorname{Sym}_n(\R) \colon A \succ 0\}$ is an open convex cone in $\R^{n \times n}$. While the Euclidean metric yields flat zero curvature, the natural geometry arises from the transitive Lie group action of $GL(n)$ via congruence $A \mapsto G A G^T$.

\begin{definition}[Affine-Invariant Metric]
For $A \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^n$ and tangent vectors $U, V \in T_A \mathcal{S}_{++}^n \cong \operatorname{Sym}_n(\R)$, the \emph{Affine-Invariant Riemannian Metric} is defined by:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:affine_metric}
    g_A^{\mathrm{AI}}(U, V) \coloneqq \Tr\left( A^{-1} U A^{-1} V \right) = \Tr\left( A^{-1/2} U A^{-1} V A^{-1/2} \right).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Curvature of the Affine-Invariant Cone]
\label{thm:cone_curvature}
Endowed with the affine-invariant metric $g^{\mathrm{AI}}$, the space $(\mathcal{S}_{++}^n, g^{\mathrm{AI}})$ is a complete, simply connected Hadamard manifold (symmetric space $GL(n)/O(n)$) with non-positive sectional curvature. For linearly independent $U, V \in T_A \mathcal{S}_{++}^n$, with $\tilde U \coloneqq A^{-1/2} U A^{-1/2}$ and $\tilde V \coloneqq A^{-1/2} V A^{-1/2}$,
\begin{equation}
    K(U, V) = \frac{-\frac{1}{4} \|[\tilde U, \tilde V]\|_F^2}{g^{\mathrm{AI}}_A(U,U)\, g^{\mathrm{AI}}_A(V,V) - g^{\mathrm{AI}}_A(U,V)^2} \le 0,
\end{equation}
with equality exactly when $\tilde U$ and $\tilde V$ commute.
The unique geodesic connecting $A, B \in \mathcal{S}_{++}^n$ is given explicitly by:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cone_geodesic}
    \gamma(t) = A^{1/2} \left( A^{-1/2} B A^{-1/2} \right)^t A^{1/2}, \quad t \in [0, 1].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $A(t)$ be a curve in $\mathcal{S}_{++}^n$ with $A(0) = A$ and $\dot{A}(0) = U$. The Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ associated with \eqref{eq:affine_metric} is given by:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_U V = D V[U] - \frac{1}{2}(U A^{-1} V + V A^{-1} U),
\end{equation}
where $D V[U]$ denotes the directional Euclidean derivative. The geodesic equation $\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}} \dot{\gamma} = 0$ reduces to:
\begin{equation}
    \ddot{\gamma}(t) - \dot{\gamma}(t) \gamma(t)^{-1} \dot{\gamma}(t) = 0.
\end{equation}
Substituting $\gamma(t) = A^{1/2} \exp(t \log(A^{-1/2} B A^{-1/2})) A^{1/2}$ satisfies this differential equation with boundary conditions $\gamma(0) = A$ and $\gamma(1) = B$. Standard curvature tensor computation on symmetric spaces (see \cite{bhatia2009positive}) gives the numerator $R(U,V,V,U) = -\frac{1}{4} \|[\tilde U, \tilde V]\|_F^2 \le 0$; dividing by $|U \wedge V|^2_{g^{\mathrm{AI}}}$ gives $K(U,V)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Bures-Wasserstein Optimal Transport Metric}
In quantum mechanics and optimal transport of Gaussian probability measures $\mathcal{N}(0, A)$ (see Bhatia~\cite{bhatia2009positive} and Villani~\cite{villani2009optimal}), the relevant Riemannian structure is the \emph{Bures-Wasserstein metric}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:bures_metric}
    g_A^{\mathrm{BW}}(U, V) \coloneqq \frac{1}{2} \Tr(\mathcal{S}_A(U) V),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_A(U)$ is the unique symmetric solution to the continuous Lyapunov equation $A \mathcal{S}_A(U) + \mathcal{S}_A(U) A = U$. (With this normalization, in dimension one $g^{\mathrm{BW}}_a(u,u) = u^2/(4a)$, which is the metric of the distance below.) The resulting geodesic distance is:
\begin{equation}
    d_{\mathrm{BW}}^2(A, B) = \Tr(A) + \Tr(B) - 2 \Tr\left((A^{1/2} B A^{1/2})^{1/2}\right),
\end{equation}
which is a non-negatively curved metric space (an Alexandrov space with curvature $\ge 0$; see Takatsu~\cite{takatsu2010wasserstein}).

\subsection{The Fixed-Rank Variety}
\label{subsec:fixed_rank}
Let $\mathcal{M}_r \coloneqq \{A \in \R^{m \times n} \colon \rank(A) = r\}$ where $1 \le r < \min(m, n)$.

\begin{proposition}[Tangent Space Projection on $\mathcal{M}_r$]
\label{prop:tangent_proj_mr}
Let $A \in \mathcal{M}_r$ with thin singular value decomposition $A = U \Sigma V^T$, where $U \in \R^{m \times r}, \Sigma \in \R^{r \times r}, V \in \R^{n \times r}$. The tangent space $T_A \mathcal{M}_r$ is an embedded subspace of dimension $r(m + n - r)$, given by:
\begin{equation}
    T_A \mathcal{M}_r = \{ U M V^T + U_p V^T + U V_p^T \colon M \in \R^{r \times r}, \ U_p^T U = 0, \ V_p^T V = 0 \}.
\end{equation}
The orthogonal projection operator $\mathcal{P}_{T_A \mathcal{M}_r} \colon \R^{m \times n} \to T_A \mathcal{M}_r$ under the Euclidean metric is:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:tangent_proj_formula}
    \mathcal{P}_{T_A \mathcal{M}_r}(Z) = U U^T Z + Z V V^T - U U^T Z V V^T.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The Tensor-Train (MPS) Riemannian Variety}
\label{subsec:tensor_train_manifold}
For tensor spaces $\R^{d_1 \times \dots \times d_k}$, the dimension $D = \prod_{\alpha=1}^k d_\alpha$ grows exponentially. The \emph{Tensor-Train} (TT) format of Oseledets~\cite{oseledets2011tt} (the same object as open-boundary Matrix Product States) factorizes an order-$k$ tensor $\mathcal{T}$ with TT-rank $\mathbf{r} = (r_0, \dots, r_k)$ ($r_0 = r_k = 1$) via localized 3-tensors $G_\alpha \in \R^{r_{\alpha-1} \times d_\alpha \times r_\alpha}$:
\begin{align}
    \label{eq:tt_decomposition}
    \mathcal{T}_{i_1, \dots, i_k} &= G_1(i_1) G_2(i_2) \cdots G_k(i_k) \\
    &= \sum_{\alpha_1=1}^{r_1} \cdots \sum_{\alpha_{k-1}=1}^{r_{k-1}} G_1(1, i_1, \alpha_1) G_2(\alpha_1, i_2, \alpha_2) \cdots G_k(\alpha_{k-1}, i_k, 1). \notag
\end{align}
Let $\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}} \subset \R^{d_1 \times \dots \times d_k}$ denote the set of tensors of fixed TT-rank $\mathbf{r}$, i.e.\ whose $\alpha$-th unfolding (rows indexed by $(i_1,\dots,i_\alpha)$, columns by $(i_{\alpha+1},\dots,i_k)$) has rank exactly $r_\alpha$ for $\alpha = 1,\dots,k-1$. This set is non-empty if and only if the ranks are feasible:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:tt_feasible}
    r_\alpha \le \min\big( r_{\alpha-1} d_\alpha,\ d_{\alpha+1} r_{\alpha+1} \big), \qquad \alpha = 1, \dots, k-1.
\end{equation}
Feasibility implies $r_\alpha \le \min(\prod_{\beta \le \alpha} d_\beta, \prod_{\beta > \alpha} d_\beta)$. For instance, $\mathbf{d} = (2,2,2)$ and $\mathbf{r} = (1,3,3,1)$ is not feasible.

\begin{theorem}[Smooth Embedded Manifold Structure of Tensor-Trains]
\label{thm:tt_manifold}
Assume the ranks $\mathbf{r}$ satisfy \eqref{eq:tt_feasible}. Then the set $\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}$ is a smooth, embedded submanifold of the Euclidean space $\R^{d_1 \times \dots \times d_k}$, of dimension
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:tt_dim}
    \dim(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}) = \sum_{\alpha=1}^k d_\alpha r_{\alpha-1} r_\alpha - \sum_{\alpha=1}^{k-1} r_\alpha^2.
\end{equation}
In particular, for uniform dimensions $d_\alpha = d$ and ranks $r_\alpha = r$, $\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}) = 2 d r + (k-2) d r^2 - (k-1) r^2 = \mathcal{O}(k \, d \, r^2) \ll d^k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider the parameter multilinear mapping $\Phi \colon \prod_{\alpha=1}^k \R^{r_{\alpha-1} \times d_\alpha \times r_\alpha} \to \R^{d_1 \times \dots \times d_k}$ defined by \eqref{eq:tt_decomposition}. The representation possesses an internal non-compact Lie group gauge invariance: for any invertible matrices $M_\alpha \in GL(r_\alpha, \R)$ ($\alpha = 1, \dots, k-1$), the transformation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:tt_gauge_transformation}
    G_\alpha(i_\alpha) \mapsto M_{\alpha-1}^{-1} G_\alpha(i_\alpha) M_\alpha \quad (M_0 = M_k = 1)
\end{equation}
leaves the tensor $\mathcal{T}$ invariant. To eliminate this redundancy, we impose left-orthogonality gauge constraints on the unfolding matrices $L_\alpha \in \R^{(r_{\alpha-1} d_\alpha) \times r_\alpha}$ for $\alpha = 1, \dots, k-1$:
\begin{equation}
    L_\alpha^T L_\alpha = \Id_{r_\alpha}, \quad \text{where } (L_\alpha)_{(\beta, i_\alpha), \gamma} \coloneqq G_\alpha(\beta, i_\alpha, \gamma).
\end{equation}
This constrains the first $k-1$ cores to the Stiefel manifolds $\mathrm{St}(r_\alpha, r_{\alpha-1} d_\alpha)$, while the final core $G_k$ is unconstrained; the residual gauge freedom is $M_\alpha \in O(r_\alpha)$. Because of the gauge invariance, $\dif\Phi$ never has full rank. Consider the open set of parameters whose unfoldings all have full rank $r_\alpha$, which is non-empty under \eqref{eq:tt_feasible}. On it the group $\prod_{\alpha=1}^{k-1} GL(r_\alpha)$ acts freely, and the fibres of $\Phi$ are exactly its orbits. Hence $\ker \dif\Phi$ is the tangent space to the orbit, of dimension $\sum_{\alpha=1}^{k-1} r_\alpha^2$, and $\dif\Phi$ has constant rank equal to \eqref{eq:tt_dim}. Holtz, Rohwedder, and Schneider~\cite{holtz2012tensor} show that the image is an embedded submanifold of this dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Tangent Space Projection and Projected Gradient Flow]
\label{thm:tt_tangent_proj}
Let $\mathcal{T} \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}$ with left-orthogonal cores $G_1, \dots, G_{k-1}$ and right-orthogonal cores $\widetilde{G}_2, \dots, \widetilde{G}_k$. 
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item Any tangent vector $\delta \mathcal{T} \in T_{\mathcal{T}} \mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}$ admits a unique orthogonal decomposition into core variations:
    \begin{equation}
        \delta \mathcal{T} = \sum_{\alpha=1}^k G_1 \cdots G_{\alpha-1} \, \delta G_\alpha \, \widetilde{G}_{\alpha+1} \cdots \widetilde{G}_k,
    \end{equation}
    subject to the gauge-fixing conditions $L_\alpha^T \delta L_\alpha = 0$ for all $\alpha = 1, \dots, k-1$.
    \item The orthogonal projection $\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathcal{T}} \mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}} \colon \R^{d_1 \times \dots \times d_k} \to T_{\mathcal{T}} \mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}$ under the ambient Frobenius inner product is given explicitly by the alternating sum:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:tt_proj_formula}
        \mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathcal{T}} \mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}}(Z) = \sum_{\alpha=1}^k \mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^L(Z) - \sum_{\alpha=1}^{k-1} \mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{LR}(Z),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^L \coloneqq P_{\le \alpha-1} \otimes \Id_{d_\alpha} \otimes P_{\ge \alpha+1}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{LR} \coloneqq P_{\le \alpha} \otimes P_{\ge \alpha+1}$. Here $P_{\le \alpha}$ is the orthogonal projector of $\R^{d_1 \cdots d_\alpha}$ onto the column space of the $\alpha$-th unfolding of $\mathcal{T}$, $P_{\ge \alpha+1}$ is the orthogonal projector of $\R^{d_{\alpha+1} \cdots d_k}$ onto its row space, and $P_{\le 0} = P_{\ge k+1} = 1$.
    \item For any smooth loss functional $\mathcal{L} \colon \R^{d_1 \times \dots \times d_k} \to \R$, the continuous projected Riemannian gradient flow:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:tt_flow}
        \frac{\dif \mathcal{T}(t)}{\dif t} = - \mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathcal{T}(t)} \mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}}\left( \nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}(t)) \right)
    \end{equation}
    stays in $\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}$, and so keeps TT-rank $\mathbf{r}$, on its maximal interval of existence $[0, T_{\max})$ in $\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}$, and the objective is non-increasing: $\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}(t)) = -\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathcal{T}(t)} \mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{TT}}}(\nabla \mathcal{L})\|_F^2 \le 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Statement (i) follows from differentiating the product map \eqref{eq:tt_decomposition} and enforcing the linearized Stiefel constraints $\dif(L_\alpha^T L_\alpha) = \delta L_\alpha^T L_\alpha + L_\alpha^T \delta L_\alpha = 0$. Since each core appears linearly, the tangent space is the sum of ranges of the partial frame operators $\mathcal{U}_\alpha \colon \delta G_\alpha \mapsto G_1 \cdots \delta G_\alpha \cdots \widetilde{G}_k$. Orthogonality between modes $\alpha \neq \beta$ is established by the mixed left-right gauge: $\sum_{i_\alpha} G_\alpha(i_\alpha)^T G_\alpha(i_\alpha) = \Id$ and $\sum_{i_\beta} \widetilde{G}_\beta(i_\beta) \widetilde{G}_\beta(i_\beta)^T = \Id$. The alternating sum projection \eqref{eq:tt_proj_formula} follows from the Lubich--Oseledets--Vandereycken tangent formula \cite{lubich2015tt} (see also \cite{absil2009optimization} for the general Riemannian framework), while assertion (iii) is an immediate consequence of the chain rule $\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}(t)) = \langle \nabla \mathcal{L}, \dot{\mathcal{T}} \rangle_F = -\langle \nabla \mathcal{L}, \mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathcal{T}}} \nabla \mathcal{L} \rangle_F = -\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{\mathcal{T}}} \nabla \mathcal{L}\|_F^2 \le 0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Hamiltonian and Projected Gradient Flows on Matrices}
\label{sec:gradient_flows}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{The Toda Lattice and Continuous QR Eigenvalue Dynamics}
Discrete iterative eigenvalue algorithms arise as stroboscopic time-1 maps of continuous Hamiltonian flows (see Flaschka~\cite{flaschka1974toda}, Symes~\cite{symes1982qr}, and Deift, Nanda, and Tomei~\cite{deift1983ode}).

Let $A \in \operatorname{Sym}_n(\R)$. Any matrix $X \in \R^{n \times n}$ decomposes uniquely as $X = \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(X) + \Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(X)$, the direct sum of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ of skew-symmetric matrices and the Borel subalgebra $\mathfrak{b}$ of upper-triangular matrices. Writing $X_{<0}$ for the strictly lower-triangular part of $X$, the skew-symmetric component is $\Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(X) = X_{<0} - X_{<0}^T$, and the complementary upper-triangular component is $\Pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(X) = X - \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(X)$.

\begin{definition}[The Continuous Toda Lattice Flow]
The \emph{Toda flow} is the differential equation on $\operatorname{Sym}_n(\R)$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:toda_flow}
    \frac{\dif A(t)}{\dif t} = [A(t), \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(A(t))] = A(t) \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(A(t)) - \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(A(t)) A(t).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Isospectrality and QR Interpolation]
\label{thm:toda_qr}
Let $A(t)$ solve the Toda lattice equation \eqref{eq:toda_flow} with initial condition $A(0) = A_0 \in \operatorname{Sym}_n(\R)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item The flow is \emph{strictly isospectral}: the spectrum $\sigma(A(t)) = \sigma(A_0)$ is invariant for all $t \ge 0$.
    \item There exists an orthogonal matrix curve $Q(t) \in O(n)$ such that $A(t) = Q(t)^T A_0 Q(t)$, where $Q(t)$ satisfies the kinematic equation $\dot{Q}(t) = Q(t) \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(A(t))$.
    \item Let $\exp(t A_0) = Q(t) R(t)$ be the $QR$ factorization of the matrix exponential at time $t$. Then:
    \begin{equation}
        A(t) = Q(t)^T A_0 Q(t).
    \end{equation}
    In particular, $e^{A(t+1)} = R_1 Q_1$ whenever $e^{A(t)} = Q_1 R_1$: at integer times the flow reproduces the iterates of the unshifted QR algorithm applied to $e^{A_0}$ (not to $A_0$) \cite{symes1982qr, deift1983ode}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
To prove (i), let $A(t) = Q(t)^T A_0 Q(t)$ with $Q(0) = \Id$ and $\dot{Q}(t) = Q(t) K(t)$, where $K(t) = \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(A(t))$. Since $K(t)$ is skew-symmetric ($K^T = -K$), $Q(t)$ remains strictly orthogonal for all $t$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dif}{\dif t}(Q Q^T) = \dot{Q} Q^T + Q \dot{Q}^T = Q K Q^T + Q (-K) Q^T = 0.
\end{equation}
Differentiating $A(t)$:
\begin{align}
    \frac{\dif A(t)}{\dif t} &= \dot{Q}^T A_0 Q + Q^T A_0 \dot{Q} = -K Q^T A_0 Q + Q^T A_0 Q K \notag \\
    &= -K A(t) + A(t) K = [A(t), K(t)].
\end{align}
This exactly reproduces \eqref{eq:toda_flow}, proving (i) and (ii).

To prove (iii), define $X(t) \coloneqq \exp(t A_0)$. By the Lie-group factorization on $GL(n) = O(n) \cdot \mathcal{B}^+$, let $X(t) = Q(t) R(t)$ be the QR factorization. Differentiating with respect to $t$:
\begin{equation}
    A_0 X(t) = \dot{Q}(t) R(t) + Q(t) \dot{R}(t).
\end{equation}
Multiplying on the left by $Q(t)^T$ and on the right by $R(t)^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
    Q(t)^T A_0 Q(t) = Q(t)^T \dot{Q}(t) + \dot{R}(t) R(t)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Notice that $Q^T A_0 Q = A(t)$ is symmetric. On the right-hand side, $Q^T \dot{Q}$ is skew-symmetric, while $\dot{R} R^{-1}$ is upper-triangular. Taking the skew-symmetric projection on both sides:
\begin{equation}
    \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(A(t)) = Q(t)^T \dot{Q}(t),
\end{equation}
which implies $\dot{Q}(t) = Q(t) \Pi_{\mathfrak{so}}(A(t))$. Since $A(t) = Q(t)^T A_0 Q(t)$ satisfies the initial condition $A(0) = A_0$, by uniqueness of ODE solutions, the trajectory coincides with the continuous QR interpolation. Finally, $e^{A(1)} = Q(1)^T e^{A_0} Q(1) = Q(1)^T Q(1) R(1) Q(1) = R(1) Q(1)$, one unshifted QR step applied to $e^{A_0} = Q(1)R(1)$; since the flow is autonomous, the same holds between any times $t$ and $t+1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Projected Gradient Flows on Low-Rank Varieties}
For a smooth loss functional $\mathcal{L} \colon \R^{m \times n} \to \R$, the unconstrained gradient flow $\dot{A} = -\nabla \mathcal{L}(A)$ typically increases matrix rank. To enforce rank constraints, we project the flow onto the tangent bundle of $\mathcal{M}_r$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:projected_flow}
    \frac{\dif A(t)}{\dif t} = -\mathcal{P}_{T_{A(t)} \mathcal{M}_r}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(A(t))).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Rank Invariance and Energy Monotonicity]
\label{thm:rank_preservation}
Let $\mathcal{L} \in C^2(\R^{m \times n})$ with $L$-Lipschitz gradient. Assume $A_0 \in \mathcal{M}_r$ with smallest non-zero singular value $\sigma_r(A_0) > 0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item There exists a maximal existence time $T^* \in (0, \infty]$ such that the solution $A(t)$ to \eqref{eq:projected_flow} satisfies $\rank(A(t)) = r$ for all $t \in [0, T^*)$.
    \item The loss is non-increasing:
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\dif}{\dif t} \mathcal{L}(A(t)) = -\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{A(t)} \mathcal{M}_r}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(A(t)))\|_F^2 \le 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item If $T^* < \infty$, then $A(t)$ has a limit $A^* \coloneqq \lim_{t \to T^*} A(t)$, and it lies in the lower stratum:
    \begin{equation}
        \lim_{t \to T^*} \sigma_r(A(t)) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad A^* \in \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{r-1}.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal{M}_r$ is an embedded smooth submanifold of $\R^{m \times n}$, the projection operator $\mathcal{P}_{T_A \mathcal{M}_r}$ defined in \eqref{eq:tangent_proj_formula} is smooth on $\mathcal{M}_r$. By the Cauchy-Lipschitz (Picard-Lindel\"of) theorem on Riemannian manifolds, there exists a unique local trajectory $A(t) \in \mathcal{M}_r$ for $t \in [0, T^*)$.

By the chain rule:
\begin{align}
    \frac{\dif}{\dif t} \mathcal{L}(A(t)) &= \inner{\nabla \mathcal{L}(A(t))}{\dot{A}(t)}_F \notag \\
    &= \inner{\nabla \mathcal{L}(A(t))}{-\mathcal{P}_{T_{A(t)} \mathcal{M}_r}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(A(t)))}_F \notag \\
    &= -\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{A(t)} \mathcal{M}_r}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(A(t)))\|_F^2 \le 0,
\end{align}
where the last equality uses the self-adjointness of the orthogonal projection $\mathcal{P}^2 = \mathcal{P} = \mathcal{P}^*$.

Finally, suppose $T^* < \infty$. Since $\mathcal{P}$ is an orthogonal projection and $\nabla\mathcal{L}$ is $L$-Lipschitz, $\|\dot A(t)\|_F \le \|\nabla \mathcal{L}(A(t))\|_F \le \|\nabla\mathcal{L}(0)\|_F + L\|A(t)\|_F$. Gr\"onwall's inequality gives $\|A(t)\|_F \le (\|A_0\|_F + t\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(0)\|_F)e^{Lt}$, which is bounded on $[0,T^*)$. Hence $\dot A$ is bounded, $A(t)$ is Cauchy as $t \to T^*$, and the limit $A^*$ exists. The rank is lower semicontinuous, so $\rank A^* \le r$. If $\rank A^* = r$, then $A^* \in \mathcal{M}_r$, and the trajectory would stay in a compact neighbourhood of $A^*$ in $\mathcal{M}_r$ for $t$ near $T^*$. This contradicts the escape lemma for maximal integral curves of a smooth vector field on a manifold. Therefore $\rank A^* < r$, i.e.\ $\sigma_r(A^*) = 0$ and $A^* \in \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{r-1}$. By continuity of singular values, $\sigma_r(A(t)) \to 0$. The case $T^* < \infty$ occurs: for $\mathcal{L}(A) = \langle C, A\rangle_F$ with $A_0 = C = E_{11}$ (and $r = 1$) the flow is $A(t) = (1-t)E_{11}$, so $T^* = 1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Non-Local Partial Differential Equations on Graphons}
\label{sec:graphon_pdes}
% =========================================================================

When the ambient dimension $n \to \infty$, discrete network dynamics converge to partial differential equations on continuous kernels (see Lov\'asz~\cite{lovasz2012large}). Let $\mathcal{W}_0 \coloneqq \{W \in L^2([0, 1]^2) \colon W(x, y) = W(y, x)\}$. Recall the cut norm:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cut_norm_def_paper2}
    \cutnorm{W} \coloneqq \sup_{S, T \subseteq [0, 1]} \left| \int_{S \times T} W(x, y) \dif x \dif y \right|.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Graphon Laplacian Operator}
For a static graphon $W \in \mathcal{W}_0$, define the continuous degree function:
\begin{equation}
    d_W(x) \coloneqq \int_0^1 W(x, z) \dif z.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Graphon Laplacian]
The \emph{Unnormalized Graphon Laplacian} $\mathcal{L}_W \colon L^2([0, 1]) \to L^2([0, 1])$ is defined by:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:graphon_laplacian}
    (\mathcal{L}_W u)(x) \coloneqq d_W(x) u(x) - \int_0^1 W(x, y) u(y) \dif y = \int_0^1 W(x, y)(u(x) - u(y)) \dif y.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Dirichlet Energy of the Graphon Laplacian]
Assume in addition that $0 \le W \in L^\infty([0,1]^2)$. Then the operator $\mathcal{L}_W$ is bounded with $\|\mathcal{L}_W\| \le 2\|W\|_\infty$, self-adjoint, and positive semi-definite on $L^2([0, 1])$. Its quadratic form satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:graphon_dirichlet}
    \mathcal{E}_W(u) \coloneqq \inner{u}{\mathcal{L}_W u}_{L^2} = \frac{1}{2} \iint_{[0,1]^2} W(x, y) (u(x) - u(y))^2 \dif x \dif y \ge 0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\|d_W\|_\infty \le \|W\|_\infty$ and the integral operator with kernel $W$ has norm at most $\|W\|_{L^2} \le \|W\|_\infty$, $\mathcal{L}_W$ is bounded. It is self-adjoint because $W$ is symmetric and real. Expanding $(u(x) - u(y))^2$ and using the symmetry of $W$ gives the identity \eqref{eq:graphon_dirichlet}, which is non-negative because $W \ge 0$. Both hypotheses are needed. For $W \equiv -1$ and $u(x) = \cos 2\pi x$ one gets $\mathcal{E}_W(u) = -\frac12$. For $W(x,y) = f(x) + f(y)$ with $f(x) = x^{-1/3} \in L^2 \setminus L^\infty$, the kernel is in $L^2$ but $d_W$ is unbounded, and multiplication by $d_W$ is not bounded on $L^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Non-Local Graphon Heat Equation}
We consider the continuous diffusion equation for the graphon kernel itself under self-diffusion:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:graphon_heat}
    \begin{cases}
        \partial_t W(t, x, y) = \Delta_{\otimes} W(t, x, y), & (t, x, y) \in (0, \infty) \times (0, 1)^2, \\
        W(0, x, y) = W_0(x, y),
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_\otimes W(x, y) \coloneqq \int_0^1 (W(x, z) + W(z, y) - 2 W(x, y)) \dif z$.

\begin{theorem}[Global Well-Posedness and Semigroup Generation]
\label{thm:graphon_heat_existence}
Let $W_0 \in L^\infty([0, 1]^2)$, with degree functions $d_1(x) = \int_0^1 W_0(x,z)\dif z$, $d_2(y) = \int_0^1 W_0(z,y)\dif z$ and mean $m = \iint W_0$. The non-local heat equation \eqref{eq:graphon_heat} generates a uniformly continuous contraction semigroup $e^{t \Delta_\otimes}$ on $L^p([0, 1]^2)$ for all $1 \le p \le \infty$, and its solution is given in closed form by
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:graphon_heat_exact}
    W(t, x, y) = e^{-2t} W_0(x, y) + (e^{-t} - e^{-2t}) \big(d_1(x) + d_2(y)\big) + (1 - e^{-t})^2\, m .
\end{equation}
The coefficients are non-negative and $e^{-2t} + 2(e^{-t} - e^{-2t}) + (1-e^{-t})^2 = 1$, the middle coefficient being counted once for $d_1$ and once for $d_2$. Thus $W(t,x,y)$ is a convex combination of $W_0(x,y)$, $d_1(x)$, $d_2(y)$ and $m$, so it takes values in $[0,1]$ whenever $W_0$ does. Moreover $W(t) \to m$ uniformly as $t \to \infty$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta_\otimes = \mathcal{K} - 2\mathcal{I}$ with $\mathcal{K} W = P_1 W + P_2 W$, where $(P_1W)(x,y) = \int_0^1 W(x,z)\dif z$ and $(P_2 W)(x,y) = \int_0^1 W(z,y)\dif z$. Since $\|P_i\|_{L^p \to L^p} \le 1$, $\mathcal{K}$ is bounded with $\|\mathcal{K}\| \le 2$, so $e^{t\Delta_\otimes} = e^{-2t}e^{t\mathcal{K}}$ is uniformly continuous with $\|e^{t\Delta_\otimes}\| \le e^{-2t}e^{2t} = 1$. For the closed form, observe that $P_i^2 = P_i$ and $P_1 P_2 W = P_2 P_1 W = m$, hence $\mathcal{K}^2 W_0 = \mathcal{K} W_0 + 2m$ and $\mathcal{K} m = 2m$. On the invariant span of $\{W_0, \mathcal{K}W_0, m\}$, writing $e^{t\mathcal{K}} W_0 = W_0 + f(t)\mathcal{K}W_0 + g(t) m$ gives $f' = 1 + f$ and $g' = 2f + 2g$ with $f(0) = g(0) = 0$. Hence $f = e^t - 1$ and $g = (e^t - 1)^2$, and multiplying by $e^{-2t}$ yields \eqref{eq:graphon_heat_exact}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cut-Norm Contraction and Decay of Singular Supports]
\label{thm:heat_smoothing}
Let $W(t) = e^{t \Delta_\otimes} W_0$ solve the non-local graphon heat equation.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Cut-Norm Contraction:} The cut norm of the evolving graphon is non-increasing:
    \begin{equation}
        \cutnorm{W(t)} \le \cutnorm{W_0}, \quad \forall t \ge 0.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Singular Support Decay:} Unlike classical local parabolic equations, the bounded non-local operator $\Delta_\otimes = \mathcal{K} - 2\mathcal{I}$ does not exhibit instantaneous regularization: if $W_0$ has jump discontinuities, $W(t, \cdot, \cdot)$ remains discontinuous for every $t \ge 0$, with the singular support decaying exponentially at rate $e^{-2t}$:
    \begin{equation}
        W(t, x, y) = e^{-2t} W_0(x, y) + \mathcal{K}_t[W_0](x, y),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{K}_t[W_0] \in C^0([0, 1]^2)$ whenever the integrated degree functions $d_{W_0} \in C^0([0, 1])$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For (i), use the closed form \eqref{eq:graphon_heat_exact}. For measurable $S, T$ we have $|\int_{S \times T} d_1(x)\dif x\dif y| = |T|\,|\int_{S\times[0,1]} W_0| \le \cutnorm{W_0}$, similarly for $d_2$, and $|m| \le \cutnorm{W_0}$. By the triangle inequality,
\[
\cutnorm{W(t)} \le \big[e^{-2t} + 2(e^{-t} - e^{-2t}) + (1 - e^{-t})^2\big]\cutnorm{W_0} = \cutnorm{W_0}.
\]
By the semigroup property, $\cutnorm{W(t+s)} \le \cutnorm{W(t)}$, so the cut norm is non-increasing in $t$.

To prove (ii), write $\Delta_\otimes = \mathcal{K} - 2\mathcal{I}$, where $(\mathcal{K} W)(x, y) = \int_0^1 (W(x, z) + W(z, y)) \dif z$. By Duhamel's formula:
\begin{equation}
    e^{t \Delta_\otimes} = e^{-2t} \mathcal{I} + \int_0^t e^{-2(t-s)} \mathcal{K} e^{s \Delta_\otimes} \dif s.
\end{equation}
Because $\mathcal{K}$ is an integral operator, the convolution term $\mathcal{K}_t[W_0] = \int_0^t e^{-2(t-s)} \mathcal{K} W(s) \dif s$ inherits the continuity of the integrated marginals $d_W(x)$. However, the unperturbed initial profile $W_0$ persists scaled by $e^{-2t}$, so any jump discontinuity in $W_0$ remains present for all $t > 0$ with amplitude $e^{-2t} \Delta W_0$, vanishing asymptotically only as $t \to \infty$.
\end{proof}

\section{Curvature Flows: Ricci Flow on Dense Networks and Level-Set MCF}
\label{sec:curvature_flows}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Ollivier-Wasserstein Ricci Curvature on Graphons}
In discrete geometry, Yann Ollivier \cite{ollivier2009ricci} defined Ricci curvature along edges via optimal transport:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(x, y) \coloneqq 1 - \frac{W_1(m_x, m_y)}{d(x, y)},
\end{equation}
where $W_1$ is the 1-Wasserstein distance between neighborhood random walk steps.

For a continuous graphon $W \in \mathcal{W}_0$, define the neighborhood probability density $m_x(z) \coloneqq \frac{W(x, z)}{d_W(x)}$.

\begin{definition}[Continuous Graphon Ricci Flow]
The \emph{Graphon Ricci Flow} for edge connection densities is the non-linear evolution equation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:graphon_ricci}
    \partial_t W(t, x, y) = 2 \kappa_W(t, x, y) W(t, x, y),
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_W$ is the continuous Ollivier curvature field associated with the kernel $W$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Curvature-Controlled Decay and Growth]
\label{thm:ricci_neckpinch}
Fix a metric $d$ on $[0,1]$ with respect to which $\kappa_W$ is defined, and let $W(t)$ solve the Graphon Ricci Flow \eqref{eq:graphon_ricci} on $[0,T)$. Let
\begin{equation}
    W_0(x, y) = \begin{cases} 1, & (x, y) \in [0, 1/2]^2 \cup [1/2, 1]^2, \\ \epsilon, & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and suppose that along the flow the curvature satisfies $\kappa_{W(t)} \le -c_2 < 0$ on the bridge $B = [0,1/2]\times[1/2,1] \cup [1/2,1]\times[0,1/2]$ and $\kappa_{W(t)} \ge c_1 > 0$ inside the communities. Then for $t < T$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item on $B$, $\ \epsilon\, e^{-2Ct} \le W(t, x, y) \le \epsilon\, e^{-2 c_2 t}$, where $-C$ is any lower bound of $\kappa$. In particular the bridge decays exponentially and does not vanish in finite time;
    \item inside the communities, $W(t,x,y) \ge e^{2 c_1 t}$, so the unnormalized flow does not preserve $[0,1]$-valued graphons.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $(x,y)$, \eqref{eq:graphon_ricci} gives $W(t) = W_0 \exp\big(2\int_0^t \kappa_{W(s)}(x,y)\dif s\big)$, and the curvature bounds yield both estimates.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not established]
Three points are left open by the Proposition, and they are stated here explicitly.
(a) The flow requires a metric $d$ on $[0,1]$. For a graphon there is no canonical graph distance, and the choice matters. With the discrete metric, $W_1(m_x,m_y)$ is the total variation distance, so $\kappa \ge 0$ everywhere and no negatively curved bridge can occur.
(b) That the curvature bounds hold initially and persist along the flow is a hypothesis. It has not been proved for any explicit choice of $d$.
(c) By item (ii), the limit $W(t) \to \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2]^2} + \mathbf{1}_{[1/2,1]^2}$ (asymptotic disconnection into two communities of density $1$) requires a normalized flow, for instance $\partial_t W = 2(\kappa_W - \bar\kappa_W)W$ with a suitable mean $\bar\kappa_W$, or a truncation. We record it as a conjecture for such normalized flows.
\end{remark}
\subsection{Matrix Level-Set Mean Curvature Flow}
In \cite{silvafilho2026functional} we recalled the classical coarea formula of Fleming and Rishel, $\TV(W_A) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \mathcal{H}^1(\partial^* E_c(W_A)) \dif c$, where $E_c(u) \coloneqq \{x \in \Omega \colon u(x) > c\}$. We now dynamically evolve these level sets via the viscosity level-set Mean Curvature Flow formulated by Evans and Spruck~\cite{evans1991mcf}. To avoid conflating the flow time with the level-set threshold, we reserve $t$ for time and $c$ for the slicing parameter throughout this subsection.

\begin{proposition}[Dissipation of Level-Set Perimeter under MCF]
\label{thm:mean_curvature_coarea}
Let $\Omega = \Tor^2 = \R^2/\Z^2$ be the flat torus, so that level curves have no boundary, and let $u(t, x) \colon [0, T) \times \Omega \to \R$ solve the level-set Mean Curvature Flow
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mcf_pde}
    \partial_t u = |\nabla u| \operatorname{div}\left( \frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|} \right), \quad u(0, \cdot) = u_0,
\end{equation}
where $u_0$ is continuous. (The Evans--Spruck viscosity theory~\cite{evans1991mcf} requires continuous initial data. The step realization $W_A$ must therefore first be extended periodically to $\R^2$ and mollified, e.g.\ $u_0 = W_A^{\mathrm{per}} * \rho_\delta$. On a domain with boundary, level curves meeting $\partial\Omega$ would contribute boundary terms that depend on the boundary condition.) Assume moreover that on $[0,T)$ the solution is smooth and that, for a.e.\ $c$, the level set $\{u(t,\cdot) = c\}$ is a smooth curve moving by its curvature (no singularities or fattening). Then the total perimeter of all level curves dissipates at rate proportional to the squared mean curvature:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dif}{\dif t} \TV(u(t)) = -\int_{-\infty}^\infty \left( \int_{\partial^* E_c(u(t))} H(x)^2 \dif \mathcal{H}^1(x) \right) \dif c \le 0,
\end{equation}
where $E_c(u) \coloneqq \{x \colon u(x) > c\}$ and $H(x) = \operatorname{div}(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|})$ is the scalar mean curvature of the level curve at $x$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the coarea formula, $\TV(u(t)) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \mathcal{H}^1(\partial^* E_c(u(t))) \dif c$. (Equivalently, for smooth solutions, $\frac{\dif}{\dif t}\int_\Omega |\nabla u| = -\int_\Omega H^2 |\nabla u|$, and the coarea formula converts the right-hand side into the stated double integral.) For each regular level set $\Gamma_c(t) \coloneqq \partial^* \{u(t, \cdot) > c\}$, the classical first variation of arc-length under normal velocity $\mathbf{v} = H \nu$ gives $\frac{\dif}{\dif t} \mathcal{H}^1(\Gamma_c(t)) = -\int_{\Gamma_c(t)} H^2 \dif \mathcal{H}^1$. Integrating with respect to the slicing parameter $c \in \R$ yields the stated dissipation identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Tensor Networks: Gauge Theory, Path Integrals, and Continuous Limits}
\label{sec:tensor_networks}
% =========================================================================

In quantum many-body physics and multilinear analysis, contracted tensor chains such as Matrix Product States (MPS) provide compact representations of entangled states on discrete lattices. Here we establish the exact continuum limit of contracted tensor chains as the number of sites $k \to \infty$, demonstrating that high-order tensor contractions converge rigorously to non-Abelian path-ordered Wilson loops in gauge theory.

\subsection{Continuous Matrix Product States and Gradient Flows}
Let $\mathcal{H} = \int_0^L (\partial_x \psi^\dagger \partial_x \psi + c \psi^\dagger \psi^\dagger \psi \psi) \dif x$ be the Lieb-Liniger Hamiltonian for interacting bosons in 1D. Under the continuous Matrix Product State (cMPS) formalism (see Verstraete and Cirac~\cite{verstraete2010continuous} and Haegeman et al.~\cite{haegeman2011tdvp}), a field wave functional is parametrized by matrix functions $Q(x), R(x) \in \C^{r \times r}$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:cmps_state}
    |\Psi(Q, R)\rangle = \Tr\left( \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \int_0^L [Q(x) \otimes \Id + R(x) \otimes \psi^\dagger(x)] \dif x \right) \right) |0\rangle.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Projected Imaginary-Time Evolution on cMPS; Formal Derivation]
\label{thm:cmps_energy_flow}
Let $\theta = (Q, R)$ collect the real coordinates $\theta^\alpha$ of both matrix functions, write $\Psi = \Psi(\theta)$ for the state \eqref{eq:cmps_state} normalized to $\|\Psi\| = 1$, and let $E = \langle \mathcal{H} \rangle = \langle \Psi | \mathcal{H} | \Psi \rangle$. Assume, without proof, that $\theta \mapsto \Psi(\theta)$ is differentiable with values in the domain of $\mathcal{H}$, which requires the cMPS regularity conditions ensuring finite kinetic energy~\cite{haegeman2011tdvp}, and that the trajectory below exists. At each $\tau$ choose $\dot\theta$ to minimize the McLachlan functional~\cite{mclachlan1964variational}
\begin{equation}
    \Big\| (\Id - |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)\, \textstyle\sum_\alpha \partial_\alpha \Psi\, \dot\theta^\alpha + (\mathcal{H} - E) \Psi \Big\|^2 .
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item the minimizers are the solutions of the linear system
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:cmps_gradient_flow}
        g(\theta)\, \dot\theta = -\tfrac{1}{2} \nabla_\theta \langle \mathcal{H} \rangle, \qquad g_{\alpha\beta} \coloneqq \operatorname{Re} \langle \partial_\alpha \Psi | (\Id - |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|) | \partial_\beta \Psi \rangle,
    \end{equation}
    for both $\dot Q$ and $\dot R$. The metric $g$ (the pulled-back Fubini--Study metric, one quarter of the quantum Fisher information metric) is degenerate: the gauge transformations $Q \mapsto h^{-1} Q h + h^{-1} \partial_x h$, $R \mapsto h^{-1} R h$ with $h \colon [0,L] \to GL(r,\C)$, $h(0) = h(L)$, and the changes of phase and normalization do not change the ray of $\Psi$, and their generators lie in $\ker g$. The system is nevertheless consistent, because $\nabla_\theta\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle$ vanishes on $\ker g$. Every solution, for instance $\dot\theta = -\frac12 g^{+} \nabla_\theta \langle \mathcal{H} \rangle$ with the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse $g^+$ or the solution in a fixed gauge, gives the same evolution of the ray;
    \item along such a trajectory,
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\dif}{\dif \tau} \langle \mathcal{H} \rangle = -2\, \dot\theta^T g\, \dot\theta = -2 \big\| \Pi_{\Psi} (\mathcal{H} - E) \Psi \big\|^2 \le 0,
    \end{equation}
    where $\Pi_\Psi$ is the orthogonal projection onto the closed real span of $\{(\Id - |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)\partial_\alpha \Psi\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Formal derivation]
Since $\|\Psi\| = 1$ and $E = \langle \mathcal{H}\rangle$, $(\mathcal{H} - E)\Psi \perp \Psi$ and $\partial_\alpha \langle \mathcal{H} \rangle = 2\operatorname{Re}\langle \partial_\alpha \Psi | (\mathcal{H} - E)\Psi\rangle = 2\operatorname{Re}\langle (\Id - |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)\partial_\alpha \Psi | (\mathcal{H} - E)\Psi\rangle$. The McLachlan functional is a quadratic function of $\dot\theta \in \R^{\dim\theta}$. Its normal equations are \eqref{eq:cmps_gradient_flow}, and its minimizers are those $\dot\theta$ for which the projected velocity equals $-\Pi_\Psi(\mathcal{H} - E)\Psi$. If $v \in \ker g$, then $(\Id - |\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)\sum_\alpha \partial_\alpha\Psi\, v^\alpha = 0$, so $v \cdot \nabla_\theta\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle = 0$, which gives consistency. Gauge and phase directions do not change the ray, hence lie in $\ker g$. Finally, $\frac{\dif}{\dif\tau}\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle = \nabla_\theta\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle \cdot \dot\theta = -2\,\dot\theta^T g\,\dot\theta$, and $\dot\theta^T g \dot\theta$ is the squared norm of the projected velocity. The principle used is McLachlan's. When the tangent spaces are complex-linear, as for a holomorphic parametrization by $(Q,R)$, it agrees with the Dirac--Frenkel principle used for real-time evolution~\cite{haegeman2011tdvp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The derivation is formal. Existence of the flow on the infinite-dimensional cMPS manifold, and preservation of the regularity conditions along it, are not proved here. If $\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle$ is bounded below (as for the Lieb--Liniger Hamiltonian with $c \ge 0$), (ii) gives convergence of the energy. It does not give convergence of $\theta(\tau)$, and it does not show that limit points are stationary points of $\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle$: that would require precompactness of the trajectory modulo gauge and a Lojasiewicz-type or LaSalle-type argument. Even stationary points are variational approximations of bond dimension $r$, possibly only local optima, and approximate the true ground state only as $r$ grows.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Continuous Path-Integral Limit of Contracted Tensor Chains (\texorpdfstring{$k \to \infty$}{k -> infty})}
\label{subsec:path_integral_gauge}
We now address the global continuum limit of contracted tensor networks. Consider an order-$k$ tensor contracted in a ring (or periodic chain) of length $k \in \N$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:discrete_mps_trace}
    Z_k \coloneqq \Tr\left( A_k A_{k-1} \cdots A_1 \right), \quad A_j \in \R^{r \times r} \ (\text{or } \C^{r \times r}).
\end{equation}
The order matters: the trace is cyclic but not invariant under reversal. With the standard MPS ordering $\Tr(A_1 \cdots A_k)$, all statements below hold with the path ordering reversed, i.e.\ for the holonomy of $\mathcal{A}^T$ after transposition.
Let the ring be embedded in the continuous unit circle $\Sph^1 \simeq [0, 1]/\sim$ with discretization mesh points $s_j = \frac{j}{k}$, step size $\Delta s = \frac{1}{k}$, and let each core matrix $A_j$ be generated by an underlying continuous connection 1-form (gauge field) $\mathcal{A} \in C^1([0, 1], \mathfrak{gl}(r))$ via the infinitesimal transfer relation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:transfer_generator}
    A_j = \Id_r + \frac{1}{k} \mathcal{A}(s_j) + \frac{1}{k^2} \mathcal{R}_j(k),
\end{equation}
where $\sup_{j, k} \|\mathcal{R}_j(k)\| \le M < \infty$.

\begin{theorem}[Continuum Path-Integral Limit and Non-Abelian Gauge Invariance]
\label{thm:continuous_path_integral}
Let $\mathcal{A} \in C^1([0, 1], \mathfrak{gl}(r))$ be a continuous gauge potential on the loop $[0, 1]$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Convergence to Path-Ordered Holonomy:} As $k \to \infty$, the ordered product $A_k \cdots A_1$ converges in operator norm, with rate $\mathcal{O}(1/k)$, to the path-ordered exponential, and hence the scalar $Z_k$ converges to the non-Abelian Wilson loop:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:path_ordered_limit}
        \lim_{k \to \infty} Z_k = \Tr\left( \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \oint_0^1 \mathcal{A}(s) \dif s \right) \right),
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{P}$ denotes Dyson path-ordering:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \int_0^1 \mathcal{A}(s) \dif s \right) \coloneqq \Id_r + \sum_{m=1}^\infty \int_{0 \le s_m \le \dots \le s_1 \le 1} \mathcal{A}(s_1) \mathcal{A}(s_2) \cdots \mathcal{A}(s_m) \dif s_1 \cdots \dif s_m.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Error Bound:} Use the operator norm on $\C^{r \times r}$ and set $a_0 \coloneqq \sup_s \|\mathcal{A}(s)\|$, $a_1 \coloneqq \sup_s \|\mathcal{A}'(s)\|$ and $\|\mathcal{A}\|_{L^1} \coloneqq \int_0^1 \|\mathcal{A}(s)\| \dif s$. Then the finite-$k$ approximation error satisfies:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:dyson_error_bound}
        \left| Z_k - \Tr\left( \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \oint_0^1 \mathcal{A}(s) \dif s \right) \right) \right| \le \frac{r}{k} \left( M + \frac{a_1 + a_0^2}{2} \right) \exp\left( \|\mathcal{A}\|_{L^1} + \frac{M + a_1/2}{k} \right).
    \end{equation}
    The factor $e^{M/k}$ cannot be dropped: for $r = 1$, $\mathcal{A} \equiv 0$ and $\mathcal{R}_j \equiv M$, the error is $(1 + M/k^2)^k - 1$, which exceeds $M/k$ for every $k \ge 2$ and is at most $\frac{M}{k}e^{M/k}$.
    \item \textbf{Gauge Invariance:} Let $\Omega \in C^2([0, 1], GL(r))$ be a periodic gauge transformation ($\Omega(0) = \Omega(1)$). Under the local gauge transformation of tensor cores:
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:continuous_gauge_field}
        \mathcal{A}(s) \mapsto \mathcal{A}^\Omega(s) \coloneqq \Omega(s)^{-1} \mathcal{A}(s) \Omega(s) - \Omega(s)^{-1} \partial_s \Omega(s),
    \end{equation}
    the path-ordered holonomy transforms by conjugation:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \oint_0^1 \mathcal{A}^\Omega(s) \dif s \right) = \Omega(0)^{-1} \left( \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \oint_0^1 \mathcal{A}(s) \dif s \right) \right) \Omega(0),
    \end{equation}
    ensuring that the contracted tensor scalar invariant is strictly gauge-invariant:
    \begin{equation}
        \Tr\left( \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \oint_0^1 \mathcal{A}^\Omega(s) \dif s \right) \right) = \Tr\left( \mathcal{P} \exp\left( \oint_0^1 \mathcal{A}(s) \dif s \right) \right).
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
To prove (i) and (ii), define the continuous evolution operator $U(s) \in GL(r)$ satisfying the linear matrix differential equation:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:matrix_ode}
    \frac{\dif U(s)}{\dif s} = \mathcal{A}(s) U(s), \quad U(0) = \Id_r.
\end{equation}
By Picard--Lindel\"of theorem, $U(1) = \mathcal{P} \exp(\int_0^1 \mathcal{A}(s) \dif s)$. Next, define the discrete product sequence $U_k(j) \coloneqq A_j A_{j-1} \cdots A_1$ with $U_k(0) = \Id_r$. Then:
\begin{equation}
    U_k(j) - U_k(j-1) = (A_j - \Id) U_k(j-1) = \frac{1}{k} \mathcal{A}(s_j) U_k(j-1) + \frac{1}{k^2} \mathcal{R}_j U_k(j-1).
\end{equation}
Meanwhile, integrating \eqref{eq:matrix_ode} from $s_{j-1}$ to $s_j$:
\begin{equation}
    U(s_j) - U(s_{j-1}) = \int_{s_{j-1}}^{s_j} \mathcal{A}(s) U(s) \dif s = \frac{1}{k} \mathcal{A}(s_j) U(s_{j-1}) + \mathcal{E}_j,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E}_j = \int_{s_{j-1}}^{s_j} \big[(\mathcal{A}(s) - \mathcal{A}(s_j))U(s) + \mathcal{A}(s_j)(U(s) - U(s_{j-1}))\big] \dif s$. Using $\|\mathcal{A}(s) - \mathcal{A}(s_j)\| \le a_1 (s_j - s)$, $\|U(s) - U(s_{j-1})\| \le a_0 (s - s_{j-1}) \sup_{[s_{j-1}, s_j]}\|U\|$ and $\|U(s)\| \le \exp(\int_0^s \|\mathcal{A}\|)$, we get $\|\mathcal{E}_j\| \le \frac{a_1 + a_0^2}{2k^2} \exp\big(\int_0^{s_j} \|\mathcal{A}\|\big)$. Let $E_j \coloneqq U_k(j) - U(s_j)$ denote the discretization error. Subtracting the two recurrence relations:
\begin{equation}
    E_j = \left( \Id + \frac{1}{k} \mathcal{A}(s_j) \right) E_{j-1} + \frac{1}{k^2} \mathcal{R}_j U_k(j-1) - \mathcal{E}_j, \qquad E_0 = 0.
\end{equation}
Unrolling the recursion,
\begin{equation}
    E_k = \sum_{j=1}^k \Big[ \prod_{\ell=j+1}^{k} \Big(\Id + \frac{\mathcal{A}(s_\ell)}{k}\Big) \Big] \Big( \frac{1}{k^2} \mathcal{R}_j U_k(j-1) - \mathcal{E}_j \Big),
\end{equation}
with the product ordered as in $U_k$. Now
\[
\big\|\Id + \mathcal{A}(s_\ell)/k\big\| \le e^{\|\mathcal{A}(s_\ell)\|/k}, \qquad \|A_\ell\| \le e^{\|\mathcal{A}(s_\ell)\|/k + M/k^2}.
\]
Hence $\|U_k(j-1)\| \le \exp\big(\frac1k\sum_{\ell < j}\|\mathcal{A}(s_\ell)\| + \frac{M}{k}\big)$. The function $s \mapsto \|\mathcal{A}(s)\|$ is $a_1$-Lipschitz, so $\big|\frac1k\|\mathcal{A}(s_\ell)\| - \int_{s_{\ell-1}}^{s_\ell}\|\mathcal{A}\|\big| \le \frac{a_1}{2k^2}$. Summing over at most $k$ indices, both the $\mathcal{R}_j$-term and the $\mathcal{E}_j$-term of the $j$-th summand are bounded by their prefactor times $\exp\big(\|\mathcal{A}\|_{L^1} + \frac{a_1}{2k} + \frac{M}{k}\big)$, with prefactors $\frac{M}{k^2}$ and $\frac{a_1 + a_0^2}{2k^2}$. Summing over $j$ gives $\|E_k\| \le \frac1k(M + \frac{a_1 + a_0^2}{2})\exp(\|\mathcal{A}\|_{L^1} + \frac{M + a_1/2}{k})$, and $|\Tr X| \le r\|X\|$ yields \eqref{eq:dyson_error_bound}, proving (i) and (ii). For the example in (ii), $e^{M/k} - 1 \le \frac{M}{k}e^{M/k}$ and $(1+M/k^2)^k \le e^{M/k}$, while $(1 + M/k^2)^k - 1 \ge M/k + \binom{k}{2} M^2/k^4 > M/k$ for $k \ge 2$.

To prove (iii), let $V(s) \coloneqq \Omega(s)^{-1} U(s)$. Differentiating $V(s)$:
\begin{align}
    \frac{\dif V(s)}{\dif s} &= \frac{\dif \Omega^{-1}}{\dif s} U(s) + \Omega(s)^{-1} \frac{\dif U(s)}{\dif s} \notag \\
    &= -\Omega^{-1} \frac{\dif \Omega}{\dif s} \Omega^{-1} U(s) + \Omega^{-1} \mathcal{A}(s) U(s) \notag \\
    &= \left( \Omega(s)^{-1} \mathcal{A}(s) \Omega(s) - \Omega^{-1} \partial_s \Omega \right) \Omega^{-1} U(s) \coloneqq \mathcal{A}^\Omega(s) V(s).
\end{align}
Since $V(0) = \Omega(0)^{-1}$, by linearity $V(1) = \left( \mathcal{P} \exp(\int_0^1 \mathcal{A}^\Omega(s) \dif s) \right) \Omega(0)^{-1}$. But by definition $V(1) = \Omega(1)^{-1} U(1) = \Omega(0)^{-1} U(1)$ by periodicity of $\Omega$. Thus $\mathcal{P} \exp(\int_0^1 \mathcal{A}^\Omega(s) \dif s) = \Omega(0)^{-1} U(1) \Omega(0)$. Using the cyclicity of the trace completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gauge Fields, Holonomy, and Higher-Order PEPS]
Theorem~\ref{thm:continuous_path_integral} shows that, for cores of the form \eqref{eq:transfer_generator}, discrete tensor contraction along a one-dimensional chain converges as $k \to \infty$ to the \emph{Wilson loop} holonomy of a 1D non-Abelian gauge field. We conjecture, by analogy, that the two-dimensional generalization to Projected Entangled Pair States (PEPS) converges to a 2D Euclidean Yang-Mills or topological Chern-Simons gauge theory in the continuum limit; we do not prove this extension here. If correct, it would supply a rigorous mechanism underlying the empirically observed correlation between gauge symmetry in PEPS ans\"atze and protected topological order, but the claim remains open beyond the 1D case established above.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion and Open Problems}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =========================================================================

We have described numerical matrix algorithms, network dynamics, and tensor approximations as continuous geometric flows and non-local partial differential equations on Riemannian manifolds and graphon spaces. The established results are the classical Toda/QR, affine-invariant and fixed-rank geometry, the finite-time behaviour of projected gradient flows, the exact solution and cut-norm contraction of the graphon heat equation, and the holonomy limit of tensor rings with an explicit error bound. The Ricci-flow and level-set statements are conditional on the hypotheses stated with them, and the cMPS statement is a formal derivation.

Open problems include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Normalized Graphon Ricci Flow:} Choosing a metric on $[0,1]$ and a normalization for which curvature bounds provably persist and two-community graphons converge to disconnected limits (Remark after Proposition~\ref{thm:ricci_neckpinch}).
    \item \textbf{Tensor Ricci Solitons:} Classifying self-similar shrinkers of multilinear tensor Ricci flows on product manifolds $\Sph^{n_1} \times \dots \times \Sph^{n_k}$.
    \item \textbf{Hypergraphon Fluid Dynamics:} Investigating whether continuous 3-uniform hypergraphon flows exhibit finite-time turbulence or singularity formation.
    \item \textbf{Wasserstein Information Geometry of Transformers:} Modeling attention map evolutions as non-local McKean-Vlasov gradient flows on graphon spaces.
\end{enumerate}
