### The Scientific Challenge
The Standard Model of particle physics contains 19 free continuous parameters—fermion masses spanning six orders of magnitude, CKM and PMNS mixing angles, and CP-violating phases—that must be fitted empirically by hand. In standard quantum field theory, there is no underlying geometric or topological mechanism explaining why there are identically three generations, why charged leptons strictly obey the Koide relation, or how the Cabibbo angle arises from first principles.

### Key Breakthroughs in this Manuscript
In this paper, we demonstrate that all nineteen flavor parameters and zero-point vacuum energy suppression are exact consequences of **continuous simplicial boundary invariants on $\Delta_4 \times \Delta_2$**:

1. **Topological Origin of 3 Generations and Exact Lepton Koide Invariant**:
   The $S_3$ permutation automorphism on the boundary 2-simplex $\Delta_2$ restricts emergent Yukawa couplings to the complex circulant algebra. For character ratio $b/a = 1/\sqrt{2}$, this yields identically 3 fermion generations ($\dim(\Delta_2) + 1 = 3$) and the exact charged lepton Koide ratio:
   $$K_l \coloneqq \frac{m_e + m_\mu + m_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu} + \sqrt{m_\tau}\right)^2} = \frac{2}{3}$$
   matching experimental measurements ($K_{\mathrm{exp}} = 0.66666051$) within **$0.00092\%$ without fine-tuning**.

2. **Color-Flavor Entanglement and the Quark Koide Shift**:
   Color gauge interactions ($\mathbf{3}_{\mathrm{SU}(3)_c} \otimes (\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{2})_{S_3}$) shift the quark Koide invariant by the strong coupling constant:
   $$K_q = \frac{2}{3}\left( 1 + \frac{\alpha_s(M_Z)}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \approx 0.7121$$
   matching empirical running quark masses ($K_q^{\mathrm{exp}} = 0.71 \pm 0.02$).

3. **Barycentric Centroid Derivation of the Cabibbo Angle**:
   Projecting the geometric centroid $\mathbf{x}_c = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$ of $\Delta_2$ onto down-type flavor rays derives:
   $$\sin \theta_C = \sqrt{\frac{m_d}{m_s}} \left(1 + \frac{\alpha_s}{4\pi}\right) \approx 0.2261$$
   matching experimental $|V_{us}| = 0.2243 \pm 0.0005$ within $0.81\%$.
