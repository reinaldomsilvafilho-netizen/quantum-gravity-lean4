## 3. TECHNICAL ENGINE AUDIT

**(a) Mosco convergence / Trotter–Kato (Part I).** The theorem statement is correct classical machinery, but the proof of Theorem 3.1 explicitly restricts the operator-norm convergence of the horizon term to *"any compact subset of the interior $\mathrm{int}(\Omega)$"* — i.e., it deliberately avoids the boundary $\partial\Omega$. That is precisely the region where all the interesting physics (Gribov horizon, entropic repulsion, mass-gap saturation per Remark 3.6) lives. Nothing in Part I establishes that the discrete domains $\Omega_n$ (defined via positivity of the *discrete* Faddeev–Popov matrix) Mosco-converge to $\Omega$ *including* matching boundary/horizon structure — only the forms on the interior are treated. Global resolvent convergence (Theorem 3.2) is then asserted for all $\lambda>0$ on the strength of an argument that provably only covers the interior. This is a genuine gap, not merely a stylistic one, since Part II's entire contribution is about boundary behavior that Part I's convergence proof never actually reaches.

There is also an unaddressed formulation mismatch: Part I's discrete measure (Def. 2.1) inserts the Faddeev–Popov *determinant* $\det(\mathcal{M}_n(U))$ by hand, while the Master paper's continuum measure (Hyp. 3.1) uses the *localized* Zwanziger action with explicit ghost fields $(\bar\varphi,\varphi,\bar\omega,\omega)$. These are formally equivalent (integrating out the ghosts reproduces the determinant) but that equivalence is never verified to survive the $n\to\infty$ limit — a one-paragraph lemma is missing.

**(b) Obstacle regularity / entropic repulsion (Part II).** The linear-vanishing eigenvalue perturbation $\lambda_0(\mathcal M_A)\sim v_0\,\epsilon$ is standard and fine. Two real gaps:
- The claim that the regularized functional determinant $\det'(\mathcal M_A)=\prod_{k\ge1}\lambda_k$ is "bounded and bounded away from zero" on the modular domain is asserted, not shown. Infinite-dimensional functional determinants require an explicit regularization scheme (zeta-function, Pauli–Villars, heat-kernel) and this is exactly the step where such schemes routinely introduce anomalies or scheme-dependence in gauge theory. This deserves either a proof or an explicit hypothesis box (in the style already used elsewhere in this trilogy — the author clearly knows how to flag an assumption when needed; this is a place it should have been flagged and wasn't).
- The jump from "$\mu_{\mathrm{GZ}}(d\le\epsilon)\le C\epsilon^2$" to "$\mathrm{Cap}_1(\partial\Omega)=0$" (end of Thm 4.1's proof) is a one-line "hence" with no isocapacitary/Wiener-type inequality connecting measure decay to capacity. These two quantities are not generically comparable without additional structure (a Poincaré or Sobolev inequality relating them); the step needs either a citation to the specific capacity-measure lemma being used or an explicit derivation.

**(c) Bakry–Émery / O'Neill (Master + Part II).** The AM-GM operator inequality is correct functional calculus. The O'Neill submersion argument for $\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal M}\ge0$ is standard and correctly applied. The genuine issue is Part II's Corollary 4.4, whose **internal LaTeX label is `cor:bakry_emery_unconditional`** — a verbatim leftover of exactly the overclaiming register Correction 1 was supposed to eliminate, and, more substantively, the corollary's proof does not discharge Hypothesis 3.1(a) from the Master paper (the uniform chromomagnetic fluctuation bound $|\langle\alpha,2\star[F_A\wedge\alpha]\rangle|\le2c_0\gamma_G^2$, i.e., the actual resolution of the Savvidy instability). Part II only handles conditions (b)–(c) (the ghost-resolvent boundary singularity); condition (a) — arguably the physically hardest part of the whole curvature argument — remains an unproven hypothesis wearing the label of a "Corollary." Rename the label, and either prove (a), demote the corollary to a conditional statement matching the Master paper's own Hypothesis 3.1 framing, or explicitly state in the corollary that it discharges (b)–(c) only.

**(d) Nelson reconstruction (Part III).** The GNS setup and Nelson–Parisi–Wu isomorphism argument is a reasonable sketch, but Theorem 3.1 (OS2 reflection positivity) is stated for $F\in\mathcal A_+$ without ever specifying that $\mathcal A_+$ must be restricted to the gauge-invariant subalgebra used in Definition 2.1's GNS construction ($\mathcal A_{\mathrm{inv}}$). This is not cosmetic: the Master paper's own §8 explicitly states that *"the isolated gauge-variant transverse gluon two-point function... exhibits complex-conjugate poles violating naive spectral positivity"* — i.e., reflection positivity provably **fails** for non-gauge-invariant observables in exactly this Gribov–Zwanziger construction (that failure is the accepted mechanism for gluon confinement in the GZ literature). If Theorem 3.1's $\mathcal A_+$ is read literally as the full local field algebra restricted to $\tau\ge0$, it contradicts the Master paper's own §8. The fix is a one-line scope restriction ($\mathcal A_+ := \mathcal A_{\mathrm{inv}}\cap\{\text{supp}\,\tau\ge0\}$), but as written the two papers are in tension on a point that is genuinely important to the GZ confinement mechanism, and a referee who has read both will catch it.

**(e) Federer reach and area-law confinement (Master Thm 6.1) — dimensional/algebraic error.** Working through the proof's own stated steps:

$$\|A_{\mathrm{crit}}\|_{L^2}^2 = \tfrac12 A_{\mathrm{crit}}^2 L^3 = \frac{\pi^2 L}{2g^2N}$$

is correctly derived from $A_{\mathrm{crit}}=\pi/(g\sqrt N L)$ (verified by hand and matches dimensionally: mass$^{-1}$, since $L\sim\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-1}$). Substituting $L=2/\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$:

$$\frac{\pi^2 L}{2g^2N}\Big|_{L=2/\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}} = \frac{\pi^2}{g^2N}\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-1},$$

i.e. **one** power of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-1}$ — but the manuscript's next line states this equals $\frac{\pi^2}{g^2N}\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-2}$, i.e. **two** powers. The factor of 2 in the denominator correctly cancels against $L=2/\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$, but the exponent on $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ jumps from $-1$ to $-2$ with no justification — this looks like a straightforward algebra slip, but it is load-bearing: taking the square root of the (correct) $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-1}$ value gives $\mathrm{reach}(\Omega)\propto\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-1/2}$, not the claimed $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-1}$ used in the boxed formula and propagated into $\kappa^*$, $E_0=(\kappa^*)^2$, and the string tension $\sigma=\frac{\pi}{2}(\kappa^*)^2$. There is also a related, deeper dimensional question worth the author's attention independent of the arithmetic slip: $\mathrm{reach}(\Omega)$ is defined as an $L^2(\Sigma,d^3x)$-configuration-space distance of a mass-dimension-1 field, which by dimensional analysis carries mass dimension $-1/2$, not the mass dimension $-1$ of a spacetime length — yet it is then used directly as a spacetime radius $r_{\mathrm{core}}\ge1/\kappa^*$. Either an implicit metric-normalization convention is being suppressed (plausible, but should be stated explicitly), or this reflects the same underlying exponent error. Given that Theorem 6.1 is explicitly named as one of the five central engines, this needs the author's own independent re-derivation with units tracked at every line before resubmission — I recommend not taking my correction at face value either, and instead literally redoing the dimensional bookkeeping symbol-by-symbol.

**Numerical verification tables (all four papers).** Every "PASS ... $<10^{-12}$" style entry is presented with the same visual authority as the analytic theorems, but these are necessarily finite-dimensional/toy-model stand-ins for infinite-dimensional continuum statements (there is no way to numerically evaluate $\mathrm{Ric}_\infty(\Omega)\ge K_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ on the literal infinite-dimensional gauge orbit space to $10^{-12}$). None of the four papers state this limitation next to the tables. Given how carefully the author now hedges the analytic hypotheses (Hyp 3.1, Remark 3.6, Remark 6.2), the same discipline should be applied here: add one sentence per table clarifying that these are finite-dimensional/truncated model verifications of the underlying algebraic identities, not independent numerical proofs of the continuum theorems.
